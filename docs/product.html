Concept device definition

ICU Ventilator

A critical care ventilator defined from the operator inwards: seven modes across adult, paediatric and neonatal patients, a priority-coded alarm system, and a display the clinician arranges. Specified in full, realised as an interactive simulation, never built.

ClassCritical care ventilator

Concept only This describes a hypothetical device. No such ventilator has been manufactured, verified, validated or certified, and none of this is a claim about a product that can be bought or used. Every clinical value is illustrative. The standards named below are the conventions the concept was written against; no conformity assessment has been performed. Nothing here is suitable for clinical use or for informing patient care.

This is a device definition that was taken seriously enough to be run. Rather than a slide deck describing a ventilator, the concept was specified as requirements, analysed for hazards, and then executed — so that every claim about how it behaves could be checked by operating it.

What follows is the product: what the device would deliver, what it would measure, how it would alarm, how a clinician would work it, and where the definition deliberately stops. Where a number appears, it is a number the concept actually specifies, not an aspiration.

Intended use, as the concept defines it

Continuous or intermittent ventilatory support for patients requiring mechanical ventilation, in a critical care environment, operated by trained clinical staff. The concept spans invasive ventilation of adult, paediatric and neonatal patients, with the permitted range of every setting bounded by the patient category selected at setup.

It is a definition, not a device. It has no hardware, no pneumatics, no circuit and no regulatory status — see where the concept stops below, which is as much a part of the product as what it contains.

Ventilation

Seven modes · mandatory core, licensable extensions

Modes are specified by what they guarantee to the patient, because that is the only description that survives contact with a lung that does not cooperate. A volume mode guarantees the volume and lets pressure follow; a pressure mode guarantees the pressure and lets volume follow. The concept says which, and to what tolerance.

Breath delivery

Triggering
Flow trigger, operator-set sensitivity. One patient effort initiates exactly one supported breath — a sustained effort does not stack them
Apnea backup
12 s. Every patient-triggered mode delivers a mandatory breath if no effort is detected. A mode that ventilates only on effort must not stop when effort ceases
Inspiratory flow patterns
Square, decelerating, accelerating, sine. Changing pattern alters distribution and peak pressure, never the delivered dose — the same tidal volume within ±2 %
Rise time and pause
Operator-set inspiratory rise and end-inspiratory pause, with an I:E ratio derived and displayed rather than entered twice

Deliberately out of scope

Proportional assist, neurally adjusted ventilatory assist and closed-loop adaptive support are excluded by decision, not by omission. These are vendor-proprietary control algorithms; a concept that reproduced their logic could be verified against no published specification, and the resulting behaviour would be a guess wearing a trademark. The exclusion is enforced by the build so it cannot erode feature by feature.

Monitoring

Nineteen values · waveforms, loops and derived mechanics

The concept treats the display as an instrument rather than a readout. Pressure, flow and volume are available as continuous traces; the clinician selects which numbers occupy the numeric field, and each carries a scale showing where the value sits between its bounds — because a number alone says what it is, not whether that is high.

Waveforms
1 to 6 traces from pressure, flow and volume, independently scaled, sweep 5 / 10 / 15 / 20 / 30 s
Loops
Pressure–volume and flow–volume for the last complete breath
Derived mechanics
Static compliance, driving pressure, plateau pressure, intrinsic PEEP, peak flow, rapid shallow breathing index
Freeze and measure
Two cursors with a delta per trace and in time. Ventilation, measurement and alarms continue underneath a frozen display

Hold maneuvers

An inspiratory hold to 5 s and an expiratory hold to 15 s, each releasing itself at its maximum whether or not anyone is watching. A hold suspends ventilation to obtain a measurement, so the measurement is presented on the main display and stays there until dismissed: plateau pressure, driving pressure and static compliance from the inspiratory hold; intrinsic PEEP from the expiratory one.

Oxygen

A 100 % oxygen override for 120 s, started and ended from the same control, with the remaining time shown on it. The override is bounded rather than latching, and never modifies the set FiO₂ — an override that changed the setting behind it would leave the patient on a value nobody selected once it was forgotten.

Alarm system

Ten conditions · three priorities · audible and visual

Priority is carried by the sound itself, so it can be recognised from across the bed space without looking at the device. Pulse count and repetition rate both change with priority, following the pattern shape IEC 60601-1-8 describes.

High priority
5 pulses · every 2.6 s
High airway pressure · Low airway pressure · Apnea · Oxygen supply
Medium priority
3 pulses · every 7 s
High / low minute volume · Low exhaled tidal volume · High rate · Battery
Low priority
2 pulses · every 20 s

Bursts drawn to scale across a one-second window. Where several conditions are active, only the highest priority is annunciated — several at once produce noise from which no priority can be read.

Silencing, and the difference that matters

Two separate controls, because they are two separate things. Conflating them leaves the clinician unsure which they pressed, and that matters most in the case where they believe sound is coming back and it is not.

A pause silences the conditions present when it was pressed. A condition of higher priority arriving afterwards was never silenced by anyone, so the pause ends and it is heard. Deliberate audio-off is unaffected: that was a decision about the device, not about a condition. Restoring audio is never gated — guarding the direction that restores a safeguard only teaches the clinician to dismiss the guard that matters.

Visual annunciation is independent of every audio state, and of the screen lock. Alarm limits are set per condition, or derived from present measurements by an autoset that proposes rather than applies: every derived value is clamped and shown for review before anything changes.

Operating the device

Where the interface deliberately slows the clinician down

Confirmation is spent where a mistake is expensive and withheld where it is not. A guard that fires constantly is a guard the clinician learns to dismiss, so the concept is explicit about which actions earn one.

  • Settings are staged. While ventilating, edits accumulate in a pending set and reach the patient only when accepted — one acknowledgement for a batch, not one per keypress.
  • The confirmation itemises. Each changed value is named with its before and after, so what is being accepted is visible at the moment of accepting it.
  • Hazardous transitions are confirmed. Starting, stopping, changing mode, changing patient category and turning alarm audio off. Turning audio back on is not.
  • The setup lockout is narrow. Only controls that bound a setting range — patient category, demographics, feature configuration — lock during ventilation, because changing them would move the bounds underneath the clinician. Nothing else locks.
  • Urgent actions are never more than one press away. Stop, 100 % oxygen and capture are present on the status bar from every screen. A control reached through a menu is reached too late.
  • Screen lock guards against contact, not against reading. Released by a deliberate 1.2 s hold rather than a tap, for transport and cleaning. The display stays fully readable and alarm annunciation is never suppressed by it.
  • Nothing resumes from memory. The device always powers up into standby with nothing being delivered. A device that appeared to resume ventilation from stored state would be showing delivery that is not occurring.

The display is arranged by the clinician

Panels move between and within columns, dividers resize them, panels hide and return, and four named arrangements apply a complete layout in one press. Sizes are proportions rather than fixed dimensions, so an arrangement survives a change of screen. Resizing takes from one side and gives to the other, so the layout cannot creep as it is adjusted; panels hold a minimum size and the last one cannot be hidden, because a panel shrunk to nothing is indistinguishable from one that has failed.

Rearranging is a mode the clinician selects. During ventilation their hand is on the display continuously, and a layout that reorganises itself on a stray touch is worse than one that cannot be changed. Ventilation, measurement and alarm annunciation all continue while it is on. A separate display-scale selection covers reading distance, from arm’s length to across the bed space.

Records

Event log · retention specified per class

Every alarm transition and every clinician action is recorded: setting changes with their before and after, mode changes, state transitions, maneuvers, captures, self tests, and each change of alarm audio or oxygen state. The log is filterable by category and severity, and exportable.

Retention is specified per class rather than as a single ceiling: 200 alarm events and 1000 events of every other kind, ageing out independently. Under one shared cap a busy afternoon of adjustments would push the morning’s alarms out of the record, and the alarms are the part worth reconstructing after an event.

Configuration persists across a restart; the temporary audio pause deliberately does not, since a suspension that outlived a restart would be an off switch wearing a countdown. What is held is disclosed to the operator, with the space it occupies and how full each retention class is, and can be cleared behind a confirmation that names the scope.

Editions and configuration

Three editions · four capabilities that can never be switched off

The concept assumes a device sold once and extended by licence, so that a hospital buys the depth it practises at and can widen it later without replacing the unit.

Licensing is a commercial boundary and never a safety one. Core modes, the alarm system, self tests and the event log are mandatory: they stay enabled whatever a licence says, so a malformed or tampered configuration cannot switch off a safety function. Capabilities not licensed are absent from the device rather than greyed out, so an unlicensed mode is unreachable rather than teasing.

Design basis

Specification, hazard analysis and traceability, carried in the device

Requirements are written to INCOSE characteristics — necessary, singular, unambiguous, complete, feasible and verifiable — with the device as subject and rationale kept out of the statement. The characteristics that can be checked mechanically are checked on every build: exactly one shall per requirement, no vague or unverifiable terms, and every traceability link resolving in both directions. A requirement that fails those checks does not ship.

Hazard analysis follows ISO 14971 and design-FMEA structure: for each item and its function, the failure mode, effect, cause, the controls in place and the residual position. Severity, occurrence and detection are scored 1–10 and RPN is computed — but used only to order the file for review, never as an acceptance criterion, because a high-severity failure stays unacceptable however rarely it occurs. Every hazard resolves to at least one mitigating requirement, and every requirement to at least one hazard and one test.

How the concept was proved

The specification was executed rather than only written

A device definition that is only prose can assert anything. This one was built as a working simulation, so its behaviour could be operated and measured — which is the only way a claim like “the frozen display does not change” or “high priority sounds five pulses every 2.6 seconds” can be shown rather than stated.

The lung the concept is exercised against

Paw = PEEP + V/C + Q·R − Pmus

Breaths are computed from a single-compartment lung model at 50 Hz, not replayed from recordings. Exhalation is passive exponential decay with time constant τ = R · C. Because the physics is integrated rather than scripted, device behaviour emerges: a stiff lung raises pressure for the same volume, and gas trapping produces intrinsic PEEP at a high rate without either being coded as a case.

Four illustrative lungs — normal, ARDS, COPD and pulmonary fibrosis — and four levels of spontaneous effort, across three patient categories, give the concept something to be wrong against. Verification runs at two levels: the delivery logic and every safety decision are tested directly, and above them the assembled device is operated as a clinician would operate it, with the display and the audible output measured rather than assumed.

Where the concept stops

Undefined by decision — and therefore not claimed

A concept that pretends to be complete is more dangerous than one that names its edges. The following are outside this definition entirely, and nothing above should be read as covering them.

  • All hardware. No pneumatics, blender, valves, sensors, expiratory cassette, patient circuit, humidification, enclosure, power supply or battery is defined. Power and battery appear in the interface only as indications, and are marked as simulated.
  • Gas delivery and mixing. FiO₂ is a setting and an oxygenation input, not a modelled blender. Oxygen supply is a monitored condition, not a specified subsystem.
  • Patient monitoring hardware. SpO₂ is synthetic and derived; on a real device it comes from a separate oximetry module. It shows the shape of a relationship, not a predicted value.
  • Lung model fidelity. Single compartment only. Regional heterogeneity, recruitment and derecruitment, and pendelluft are not represented.
  • Clinical validation. No value here has been compared against measured patient data. Compliance, resistance, saturation and every derived mechanic are illustrative, and the four lungs are labelled as such throughout.
  • Regulatory and quality processes. No design history file, no verification and validation protocols, no risk management report, no clinical evaluation, no post-market surveillance, no notified body involvement.

If the concept were taken further

Capnography and EtCO₂. High-flow oxygen therapy, which needs a flow-source model rather than a pressure-source one. Sigh and manual breaths, automatic tube compensation, P0.1, NIF/MIP and nebuliser synchronisation. Trend graphing over time in place of the present capture-and-compare view. And design documents as structured data alongside the requirements, hazards and test cases, completing the documentation set.
